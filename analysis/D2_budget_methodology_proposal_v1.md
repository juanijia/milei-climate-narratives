## 0. What this analysis has to deliver for the paper

The draft's Table 2 currently specifies the budgetary row as "Budget reductions affecting science, environment and sustainability programmes." That formulation is not yet an analytical claim — it describes a measurement. For the paper's argument to work, the budgetary analysis has to answer three questions, in ascending order of theoretical payoff:

1. **Descriptive.** How much did environmental and public-science spending fall under Milei, in real terms?
2. **Comparative (the gap you identified in FARN).** Did it fall *more than* the general fiscal contraction and more than other policy areas? Austerity that cuts everything by 28% is not policy dismantling — it is macroeconomics. *Selective* retrenchment is dismantling.
3. **Interpretive.** Does the pattern of selectivity track the discursive priorities identified in Corpus A? That is: are the areas most heavily cut the same areas Milei's rhetoric delegitimizes (regulation, expertise, universities, multilateral commitments), while areas his rhetoric valorizes (security, intelligence, extractive facilitation) are protected or expanded?

Question 3 is what makes this a *paper* rather than a budget monitor. It is also the join between D1 and D2, and it is only reachable if the analysis is comparative by construction. Everything below is designed backwards from it.

## 1. How FARN did it — exact reconstruction

I reconstructed FARN's method from the *Metodología* sections and the annex tables of both reports. It is a competent and replicable design, and we should stay compatible with it so our figures can be cross-validated.

**Data source.** Presupuesto Abierto (Secretaría de Hacienda), supplemented by INDEC (IPC) and the Ley de Presupuesto for projected years.

**Temporal frame.** 2017–2026. The 2017 start is not substantive: it is imposed by the availability of a homogeneous INDEC IPC series with December 2016 base, chosen explicitly to avoid splicing series with different bases.

**Which "budget" figure.** Annual **execution** (*devengado*) for 2017–2025; **crédito vigente** as of 31/01/2026 for the current year.

**Base year for comparison.** **2023** — chosen because it was the last year with a Budget Law approved by Congress. Every subsequent year has run under a *presupuesto prorrogado*, with credits set and reallocated by the Executive via DNU and Decisiones Administrativas.

**Three indicators.**
- *Porcentaje de ejecución* = devengado ÷ vigente at fiscal-year close (measures under-execution).
- *Variación real* vs 2023, deflating by the annual average IPC; for 2026, using the inflation projection embedded in the Ley de Presupuesto 2026.
- *Participación* = the item's devengado ÷ total national budget devengado (measures relative priority independent of the overall contraction).

**Unit of analysis.** Overwhelmingly the **programme / entity** level — hand-picked *partidas*: Subsecretaría de Ambiente, APN, FNECBN (Fondo de Bosques), Servicio Nacional de Manejo del Fuego, Sistema de Áreas Marinas Protegidas, Cuenca Matanza-Riachuelo, energías renovables y eficiencia energética, SMN, INPRES, INAI, subsidios a la oferta y a la demanda de hidrocarburos, SIDE, Deuda Pública.

**Where FARN does compare across areas — and where it stops.**

FARN is not silent on comparison, but it goes only one level deep. Both reports include a table of real variation **by *finalidad*** — the five top-level categories only:

| Finalidad (2025 vs 2023, real) | FARN 2026 report | FARN 2025 report (2024 vs 2023) |
|---|---|---|
| Servicios Económicos | −62.8% | −42.8% |
| Administración Gubernamental | −37.6% | −43.7% |
| Deuda Pública | −34.3% | −15.2% |
| Servicios de Defensa y Seguridad | −21.7% | −19.6% |
| Servicios Sociales | −19.9% | −22.9% |
| **Presupuesto total** | **−28.8%** | **−26.3%** |

And *Gráfico 28* ranks the environmental partidas against SIDE, Deuda Pública and the total budget.

**The three limitations this leaves us.**

1. **The comparison stops at *finalidad*, never reaching *función*.** Five categories are too coarse to be analytically useful: "Servicios Sociales" bundles pensions, hospitals and universities into a single number dominated by social security. The Argentine classifier has ~30 *funciones*, and it is at that level that Ecología, Salud, Educación, Ciencia y Tecnología become directly commensurable. **This is precisely the gap you identified, and it is fixable with data you already have.**
2. **The comparators in *Gráfico 28* are advocacy-selected, not exhaustive.** SIDE and debt service are rhetorically effective foils, but a reviewer will ask: compared to *all* areas, where does environment actually rank? FARN cannot answer that; we should.
3. **No formal measure of *relative* retrenchment.** FARN reports environment at −80.8% and the total at −28.8% and leaves the reader to do the subtraction. We should construct the excess explicitly and defend it as an indicator.

FARN's design is otherwise sound and we adopt its core (devengado, 2023 base, IPC average, participación) unchanged.

## 2. Understanding *which* budget is the correct budget

This section exists because the choice is not obvious and a reviewer will test it.

### 2.1 The five stages of a credit

Under Ley 24.156 and the MCPSPN, a budget line passes through: **crédito inicial** → **crédito vigente** → **comprometido** → **devengado** → **pagado**. Each stage answers a different question.

| Stage | What it measures | Analytical use here |
|---|---|---|
| Inicial | What the Executive tabled at the start of the year | Declared priority |
| Vigente | Inicial ± in-year modifications (DNU / Decisión Administrativa) | Revealed priority after discretionary reallocation |
| Devengado | Obligations actually accrued | **Realised policy effort** |
| Pagado | Cash out the door | Liquidity, not policy |

**Our choice: *devengado* for closed years (2023, 2024, 2025); *crédito vigente* for 2026.** *Devengado* is the only stage that measures what the state actually did, and it is the stage at which under-execution — a deliberate, decree-free method of dismantling — becomes visible. For 2026 no execution exists yet, so *vigente* is the only defensible figure, and it matches FARN.

### 2.2 The *prórroga* regime is itself a finding, not just a technicality

Argentina has had **no Budget Law approved by Congress for 2024, 2025 or 2026** (successive prórrogas of the 2023 law). Three consequences matter for the paper:

- The base year must be **2023**, the last congressionally-authorised allocation — not a technical convenience but the last point at which spending priorities carried legislative authorisation.
- **Crédito inicial loses its normal meaning.** Under a prórroga, the "initial" figures are set by the Executive, not by Congress. The inicial→vigente gap therefore measures purely executive discretion.
- FARN documents the specific instruments: DNU 186, 425, 849 and DA 10, 23, 24, 29, 38, 39 in 2025; DNU 280, 594, 656, 1104 and DA 284, 470, 858, 861, 910, 1015, 1018, 1022 in 2024. **These are Corpus B documents in their own right** — they belong in the decree analysis, and they are the mechanism linking the budgetary and administrative rows of Table 2. Recommend we log them in the Corpus B document register regardless of what we do with the numbers.

### 2.3 Institutional coverage — state it explicitly

- **Administración Nacional** = Administración Central + Organismos Descentralizados + Instituciones de Seguridad Social. This is FARN's implicit universe and Presupuesto Abierto's default.
- **Sector Público Nacional** additionally includes state enterprises, trust funds (*fondos fiduciarios*) and other entities.

**Our choice: Administración Nacional**, for comparability with FARN and because environmental and scientific agencies sit inside it. This must be declared in the methods section, because the dissolution of *fondos fiduciarios* under the Ley Bases moved money across this boundary — an artefact we must check for rather than discover in review.

### 2.4 The classifiers, and what each can and cannot see

Per the MCPSPN 2026, seven **primary** classifications are recorded directly (institucional, rubros de recursos, objeto del gasto, geográfica, tipo de moneda, fuente de financiamiento, categoría programática) and three are **derived** by conversion tables (naturaleza económica, finalidades/funciones/subfunciones). Four are relevant to us:

| Classifier | Question | Strength | Failure mode under Milei |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Finalidad–Función** (derived) | What for? | Homogeneous 2004→2026; survives every ministry reshuffle; converts to IMF GFS / COFOG (new *subfunciones* map directly to MEFP-2014 Cuadro A8.6) | Coarse; environmental activity is scattered across several functions; re-classification between functions can mask or mimic a cut |
| **Institucional** (jurisdicción / entidad / SAF) | Who spends? | The literal unit of state capability; same unit as decrees and organigramas | Series **breaks** at the 2023–24 restructuring; requires a documented concordance |
| **Categoría programática** (programa / actividad) | Which policy? | Policy *instruments* — the currency of the dismantling literature (Bauer et al.) | Codes renumbered/merged; no natural comparator universe |
| **Objeto del gasto** (inciso) | On what? | Personnel (inc. 1) vs real investment (inc. 4) — distinct capability signatures | Secondary cut only |

**One substantive observation from the classifier itself.** In the MCPSPN, environment is **4.4 Ecología y desarrollo sostenible**, sitting inside *Finalidad 4 — Servicios Económicos*, alongside energy, mining and transport. It is *not* classified as a social service. This is a pre-Milei feature of the Argentine fiscal architecture, but it is worth a paragraph in the paper: the state's own accounting ontology already frames environmental protection as a subordinate economic service rather than a right or a social function — a background condition that makes the discursive move in Corpus A ("sustainability as an ideological luxury") cheaper to execute.

## 4. Indicators — formal definitions

Let $X_{a,t}$ be nominal devengado of area $a$ in year $t$; $P_t$ the annual average IPC; $T_t$ total Administración Nacional devengado.

**1. Real variation vs base year (2023):**
$$\Delta^{real}_{a,t} = \frac{X_{a,t}/P_t}{X_{a,2023}/P_{2023}} - 1$$

**2. Budget share (participación):**
$$s_{a,t} = X_{a,t}/T_t$$

**3. Relative Retrenchment Index (RRI)** — the paper's key comparative statistic:
$$RRI_{a,t} = \Delta^{real}_{a,t} - \Delta^{real}_{T,t}$$
Negative = cut *more* than the state overall. Reported in percentage points, with the full ranking of all ~30 functions so the environment's position is not cherry-picked. Equivalently expressible as a **priority ratio** $s_{a,t}/s_{a,2023}$, which is deflator-free and therefore a useful robustness check.

**4. Execution rate:** $e_{a,t} = \text{devengado}_{a,t}/\text{vigente}_{a,t}$. Under-execution is *silent dismantling* — no decree, no announcement, no legislative trace. Worth its own paragraph in the findings.

**5. Reallocation rate:** $r_{a,t} = \text{vigente}_{a,t}/\text{inicial}_{a,t}$. Measures executive discretion under the prórroga.

**6. Robustness: % of GDP.** Deflator-independent, immune to any dispute about the IPC series, and internationally legible.

- **A defensible answer to your question.** Ecología fell 64% in GDP-share terms against a total contraction of 27.5% — i.e. **more than twice the average**, ranking 7th steepest of 29 functions. That is a real, quantified, non-cherry-picked comparative claim.
- **A more honest one than pure advocacy would give.** Environment was *not* the single hardest-hit function. Housing, water and sanitation were cut more. A paper that says "environment was cut most" will be checked and falsified; a paper that says "environment was among the most heavily cut functions, at more than twice the average contraction, alongside the collapse of infrastructure and social-provision functions" is both true and more interesting — it situates environmental dismantling within a broader retreat from state provision rather than treating it as sui generis.
- **The D1↔D2 join, visible immediately.** Ciencia, tecnología e innovación at −43.2% sits directly against Corpus A's `Attack_on_Universities_and_Research_Institutions` and `Attack_on_Experts_and_Technocrats` codes. **Inteligencia at −3.1% and Defensa/Seguridad/Judicial in the −12 to −25% band, against Ecología at −64%**, is exactly the selectivity the argument needs: the coercive and sovereign core of the state is comparatively protected while its environmental and epistemic capacities are gutted. That single contrast is arguably the strongest sentence the budgetary analysis can contribute to the paper.
- **A twenty-year baseline.** The series runs to 2004, so we can show whether the Milei contraction is discontinuous or the acceleration of a trend — a question a reviewer *will* ask and which FARN never addresses.

**Known limitations of this layer, to be stated and then remedied by Phases 2–3.**

- *Under-coverage.* Función 4.4 does not capture everything environmental: SMN sits under Defensa, INPRES under science/economic functions, INTA under Agricultura, and FARN documents fire management migrating from an environmental to a security logic. The function-level number is therefore a **lower bound** on environmental retrenchment.
- *Re-classification risk.* A fall in 4.4 could in principle reflect activity moved to another function rather than eliminated. This must be checked against the institutional layer — and where it happened, the migration is itself reportable as institutional erosion (a policy losing its environmental framing).
- *Provenance of the CSV.* `serie_finfun_anual.csv` appears to be an ONP historical series, but its exact vintage, universe (Administración Nacional vs SPN) and stage (devengado vs vigente) are not documented in the file. **Before any of these numbers enter the paper, they must be re-derived from Presupuesto Abierto via the API** so that provenance is fully citable, and the FARN *finalidad*-level figures used as a validation check (our aggregation to the five finalidades should reproduce −37.6 / −34.3 / −21.7 / −62.8 / −19.9 / −28.8 within rounding).
- *GDP-share vs IPC.* The pilot above uses GDP shares; the paper's headline figures will use IPC-deflated real variation per FARN. The two will not be numerically identical (GDP-share change embeds real GDP growth) and both will be reported, the second as a robustness check.

## 6. Phase 2 — institutional layer (jurisdicción / entidad / SAF)

Added **only after** Phase 1 is validated.

**Purpose.** Function-level data cannot say *which organisation* lost capacity, and organisations are what decrees restructure. This layer is what welds the budgetary row of Table 2 to the administrative row.

**Perimeter (draft — for your approval):**
- *Environment:* ex-Jurisdicción 17 MAyDS → Subsecretaría de Ambiente; APN; ACUMAR; SNMF; FNECBN.
- *Public science / knowledge:* CONICET; universidades nacionales; INTA; INTI; CONAE; CNEA; SMN; INPRES; INDEC.
- *Comparators:* Salud; Seguridad; Defensa; SIDE; Deuda; plus the total.

**The concordance problem, and the protocol.** The 2023–2025 restructurings break the series. The protocol:

1. Extract jurisdicción/subjurisdicción/entidad/SAF codes for every year 2023–2026.
2. Build an explicit crosswalk mapping each 2023 unit to its 2024/2025/2026 successor(s), sourced to the *decree that effected the change* (Boletín Oficial).
3. Where a unit was split or absorbed, aggregate to the **lowest common denominator** that is constant across years, and document the aggregation.
4. Publish the crosswalk as a methodological annex.

The crosswalk is not overhead — it is the primary evidence for institutional downgrading, and it doubles as the document register for the administrative row of Table 2.

**Deliverables:** institutional panel on a constant perimeter; execution-rate and reallocation-rate series (under-execution is most visible at this level — e.g. APN leaving $13.7bn unspent in 2025); the annotated crosswalk.

## 8. Validation (built in, not bolted on)

1. **External reconciliation** against FARN's published *finalidad* and partida figures.
2. **Internal consistency**: function-level sums must equal institutional sums must equal the published total for every year.
3. **Deflator sensitivity**: every headline figure reported under IPC-real, GDP-share, and share-of-total. If the three disagree in direction, the finding is not robust and does not go in the paper.
4. **Boundary check**: verify that *fondo fiduciario* dissolutions under the Ley Bases did not move spending across the Administración Nacional boundary in a way that manufactures an apparent cut.
5. **Re-classification check**: for any function showing a large fall, confirm against the institutional layer that the activity was eliminated rather than relocated.
6. **Full replication package**: scripts + raw extracts + intermediate tables committed to the repo.
