**Libertarianism and the dismantling of sustainability policy: narratives and state capabilities in Milei’s Argentina**

 

Right-wing populist governments have often been linked to resistance against sustainability and climate policy, but the Argentine case since December 2023 introduces a libertarian-populist configuration which has not been studied so far. This article examines how libertarian narratives—centered on  the epistemological illegitimacy of state-coordinated action, market primacy, and deregulation—reshape sustainability policy and the public scientific capacities that underpin climate and environmental governance. Building on literature on climate-policy backlash and policy dismantling, we analyze the Argentine case from two complementary perspectives. First, we conduct a narrative analysis of official justifications and public communications to identify how sustainability policy and scientific expertise are problematized and reframed. Second, we trace changes in state capabilities by examining institutional reorganization and policy-instrument changes that affect the implementation of sustainability policy and support for public science. Empirically, we conduct document analysis of presidential discourses, executive decrees, governmental organizational changes and trace key policy decisions in energy and environment. We argue that libertarian governance can generate sustainability-policy retrenchment through a dual mechanism: discursive delegitimation of regulation and expertise, coupled with capability erosion that weakens the state’s ability to design, fund, and enforce sustainability policy —even when formal commitments remain on paper. This framework extends backlash research by specifying how libertarian ideas can operationalize policy dismantling via state transformation rather than overt authoritarian closure.
